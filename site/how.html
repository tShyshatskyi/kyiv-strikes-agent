Agents judge. Code checks.

After a night attack on Kyiv, a dozen channels report the same hits in different words, at different hours, with casualty figures that change through the morning. Counting posts counts each strike five times. Reading them by hand takes a person a day per night.

This registry hands the reading to three AI agents with separate jobs, one of which exists only to find the others' mistakes. Everything that can be checked without judgement is checked by code, so an agent's error is caught by something that cannot make the same error.

    What the auditor caught

      Limits

      1. Reports are what they are

        Telegram posts are early and sometimes corrected later. Casualty figures are the latest a source gave, not a final count.

      2. Most places have no address

        Officials usually name a district, not a street, for security reasons. Those places get no point on the map: the district is shaded instead, because a dot would be an address no source gave.

      3. The auditor is a model too

        It catches a lot, but what it still disputes after one revision is marked disputed and never counted as confirmed.

      4. Wikipedia is not a witness

        It retells the same news, so it never raises a place's confirmation. It is used only to check whole attacks and death tolls.

      5. Attacks are still split at 09:00

        One attack that runs into the morning is still filed under two dates, so a place hit at 02:00 and summarised at 09:30 can appear twice and a death toll can be counted twice. This is why no casualty total is shown. Fixing it is the next piece of work.
